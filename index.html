Read the abstractHide the abstract

Small multimodal models can misjudge emotion when words, voice, and visual cues conflict. We present EmoHarness, which runs such a model, the frozen student, inside a harness: the prompts, settings, and verification code around its calls. A human-written starting harness, the scaffold, numbers the student’s visual observations, transcript, and prosody descriptions as citable evidence and has the student weigh candidate emotions against it. In Meta-Harness Evolving (MHE), a stronger LLM, the Builder, reads the student’s errors and execution traces offline and edits the harness; static checks and validation gates decide which edits are kept, and the Builder never sees evaluation items. On 5,507 MME-Emotion items, EmoHarness raises task-macro Recognition by 7.6–13.4 points over a single video-only call for all four students and both Builders. Transcripts and prosody descriptions supply most of this boost (+5.38 to +10.71); the scaffold changes the audio-augmented call by −0.79 to +2.54, partly through extra multi-label predictions, and evolved edits add at most +0.95, although no evolved increment survives Holm correction. The harness’s emotion context also transfers to IntentQA, with small gains for three of four students, none of which survives correction. EmoHarness thus boosts small multimodal students chiefly by supplying and organizing audio-derived evidence.

How EmoHarness works

A harness is a bundle of prompts, settings files and verification code. Meta-Harness Evolving builds one offline from the student’s failures. At inference the frozen student runs the frozen harness, with no Builder in the loop.

At inference: four student calls around fixed code

  1. Observe extracts facial, bodily, scene and temporal cues from the video at 2 fps. Code indexes them as v1, v2, … next to transcript sentences and prosody phrases, a1, a2, …; later calls see only this text record.
  2. Propose lists candidate labels with confidences and the IDs of supporting and contradicting evidence. Code checks every ID against the record, then ranks the candidates.
  3. Review re-examines the ranked candidates and is verified the same way. A fixed controller then picks at most three labels from the allowed vocabulary.
  4. Explain writes an explanation that cannot change the labels.

Audio reaches the student only as text: Whisper large-v3 transcripts and openSMILE eGeMAPSv02 features turned into descriptions such as “high pitch”, never into emotion labels.

Offline: Meta-Harness Evolving

Over four generations, the Builder (GPT-5.6 Luna or GPT-6.1 Sol) reads the incumbent’s failures on a construction split, clustered by gold–prediction confusion and by cross-modal conflict, together with execution traces. It proposes up to four candidate harnesses per generation, each aimed at one failure cluster.

Candidates pass file-scope, schema, loadability and static-code checks and a proposal reviewer that rejects shortcuts such as forcing a label. Survivors run on a separate validation split, and one replaces the incumbent only if it clears every gate:

Recognition
higher than the incumbent’s
Regression
loses at most 5% of validation items already solved
Mean calls
at most 4.5 student calls per item
Reasoning proxy
within 0.02 of the incumbent

The Builder sees neither validation scores nor gate decisions, only the resulting harness. If no candidate is ever promoted, the evolved harness is the scaffold itself.

The Builder may edit

  • Prompts for the four calls
  • Settings, such as how the transcript is presented
  • Restricted verification code

Fixed throughout

  • The student’s weights
  • Perception tools and their cached outputs
  • Evidence and output schemas, and the output controller

Added evidence does most of the work

Transcript and prosody text give every student its largest gain. Audio − Video ranges from +5.38 (Qwen3.5-4B) to +10.71 points (Gemma-4-E4B), and every interval excludes zero.

The scaffold helps two students

Shared − Audio is +2.23 for Qwen3.5-4B and +2.54 for Gemma-4-E4B, partly because the scaffold predicts more labels on multi-label tasks, which recall-like scoring rewards. For the Qwen3-VL students it is +1.32 and −0.79, with intervals that include zero.

Evolution adds little on top

MHE − Shared ranges from −0.07 to +0.95 points. The largest, GPT-5.6 Luna on Qwen3-VL-4B (+0.95, 95% CI [+0.12, +1.82]), does not survive Holm correction (adjusted p = .192), and neither does any other contrast.

Transfer to held-out task types

With one exception, held-out changes are as small as seen-type changes. MHE − Shared ranges from −0.16 to +0.47 points on seen types and, for seven of the eight student–Builder pairs, from −0.14 to +0.28 on held-out types.

The exception is again GPT-5.6 Luna on Qwen3-VL-4B: +1.24 on held-out types, lifting held-out Recognition to 36.65, still below single-call Audio at 37.55. With seen-type gains this small, the held-out results say little about transfer beyond this pair.

What an evolved edit changes

Two Gemma-4-E4B items, one where an edit evolved by GPT-5.6 Luna fixes the scaffold’s error and one where it does not. Over all 5,507 items, this harness changes Shared’s label set on 169 items: 46 improve, 45 worsen and 78 score the same. Cases are selected, not representative.

Shared rates a mildly negative remark as strong negative because a confidence score, not an observed cue, ranks that label first. With the evolved edit, the review call treats the composed posture as evidence for a neutral reading, and the harness answers the reference, negative.

Video answers the correct Neutral, but once the transcript of a billing complaint is added, Audio, Shared and both evolved harnesses answer Frustration. The Luna harness keeps Frustration even though its added rule asks it to treat neutral as a serious alternative: its explanation never names Neutral and still lists crossed arms that no sampled frame shows.

Emotion context on IntentQA

Each evolved harness writes one emotion context per video, with the predicted emotion, alternatives and a four-step analysis, without seeing any question. The bare student then answers 2,134 IntentQA test questions on 567 videos from the frames, the frames plus transcript and prosody, or those inputs plus the emotion context.

Adding the emotion context raises accuracy in all six pairs for Qwen3-VL-8B, Qwen3-VL-4B and Gemma-4-E4B, by +0.23 to +1.87 points. Qwen3.5-4B loses 1.50 and 1.41 points; it also loses 2.06 points when only transcript and prosody are added.

None of the eight contrasts survives Holm correction (smallest adjusted p = .085). There is no length-matched control, so longer inputs rather than emotion content could explain these changes.

Why evolved gains stay small

The gates promoted six GPT-5.6 Luna edits and five GPT-6.1 Sol edits across the eight runs. What the analysis points to:

Validation gains shrink on evaluation

Promoted edits raised validation Recognition by up to 2.54 points. Scored the same way on the 2,963 seen-type evaluation items, the seven promoted harnesses change by −0.20 to +0.30. Each promotion picks the best of up to four candidates on 394 items, and three of the eleven rest on a single validation item.

The regression gate blocks broad edits

All twelve candidates that gained at least eight validation items (2.03 points) exceeded the 5% regression budget. The gate protects solved cases at the cost of broad edits; whether a looser gate generalizes better needs a separate test set.

Most evolved harnesses are conservative

Apart from GPT-5.6 Luna on Qwen3-VL-4B, which changes the label set on 21.6% of evaluation items, each evolved harness changes it on fewer than 4%.

Edits are instructions the student may not follow

In the failure case, the rule written for the Neutral-to-Frustration confusion is ignored. The student’s instruction following, not only the Builder’s proposals, can limit what edits achieve. Future gates could check that traces apply edits and that citations match the media.

Intended use

Benchmark emotion labels are interpretations of behavior, not measurements of private mental states or clinical conditions. This work does not validate use in diagnosis, employment screening, surveillance or educational assessment. Frames shown on this page come from public research datasets, with on-screen names removed.
